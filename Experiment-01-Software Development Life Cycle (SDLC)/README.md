# Experiment 01 – Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

## Aim
To compare and contrast different SDLC models.

## Objectives
- Understand the concept of SDLC.
- Study different SDLC models.
- Compare their advantages and limitations.

## Theory
Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a process used to design, develop, and test software.  
Popular models include:
- Waterfall Model
- Iterative Model
- Spiral Model
- V-Model
- Agile Model

---

## SDLC Models and Definitions

### Waterfall Model
**Definition:** A linear and sequential approach where each phase (requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, maintenance) must be completed before moving to the next. Best suited for small projects with well‑defined requirements.

### Agile SDLC
**Definition:** An iterative and incremental approach that emphasizes customer collaboration, continuous feedback, and rapid delivery. Work is divided into short cycles called sprints, producing working software frequently and adapting to changes quickly.

### Iterative Model
**Definition:** Development is broken into smaller cycles. Each iteration produces a working version of the software, which is refined through repeated cycles until the final product is achieved.

### Spiral Model
**Definition:** Combines iterative development with risk analysis. Each cycle (spiral) involves planning, risk assessment, engineering, and evaluation, making it suitable for large, high‑risk projects.

### V‑Model
**Definition:** An extension of the Waterfall model where each development stage is directly associated with a testing phase. It emphasizes verification and validation at every step.

## Comparison of SDLC Models
| Parameter        | Waterfall | Iterative | Spiral | V-Model | Agile |
|------------------|-----------|-----------|--------|---------|-------|
| Development Cycle| Sequential | Incremental | Iterative | Sequential | Iterative |
| Flexibility      | Low       | Medium    | High   | Medium  | Very High |
| Risk Management  | Poor      | Moderate  | Excellent | Moderate | High |
| Customer Feedback| Late      | Early     | Continuous | At each stage | Continuous |
| Documentation    | High      | Moderate  | Moderate | High | Minimal |
| Best For         | Small projects | Medium projects | Large, risky projects | Testing-focused projects | Dynamic projects |

---

## Advantages of SDLC
- Provides structured development.
- Ensures quality and reliability.
- Defines clear stages and deliverables.
- Facilitates project tracking and management.

## Limitations
- Can be time-consuming.
- Less flexible for changing requirements.
- Some models require heavy documentation.

---

## Result
Successfully studied and compared different SDLC models.  
Each model has unique strengths and limitations, and the choice depends on project requirements.
